## GEA Context

GEA is not framed as a generic jewelry rental business.

Across the live site and supporting doctrine, it is positioned as:

- a high-design jewelry access system
- access-first, not ownership-first
- fashion-house coded rather than generic ecommerce
- editorial, controlled, and philosophically distinct
- premium, even when speaking about operations or infrastructure

The core ideas repeated across sources are:

- access is luxury
- ownership is the old model
- presence over possession
- experience over accumulation
- beauty without burden

The emotional and strategic promise behind that positioning is consistent:

- luxury should liberate, not burden
- the customer is not just buying jewelry, she is buying presence, confidence, visual authority, and alignment
- sustainability is framed as structural elegance and intelligent design, not as preachy moral branding
- the brand wants to read as a house, an atelier, a system, or a world - not a generic storefront

That matters because whatever we build internally should still come from that worldview, even if it is purely operational.

## Business Model Snapshot

The GEA business model is already quite defined in language, even if the company is still in an active build stage.

What seems settled across the materials reviewed:

- GEA operates as a membership-based access model
- the current logic centers on a 5-piece tier and a 10-piece tier
- members receive one curated shipment per cycle
- one piece per cycle can be kept as a favorite
- additional keeps are available at a member discount
- the argument is not "cheap jewelry" or "budget luxury"
- the argument is that access is smarter than ownership because it creates more variety, more visual impact, and less burden

In other words, GEA is not trying to make ownership more affordable. It is trying to make ownership feel outdated.

## Current Stage

GEA appears to be in an active buildout stage rather than a fully mature operating state.

Important current realities:

- the membership logic is already live in language
- the trust architecture is already strongly defined
- some internal systems already exist in the workspace
- some of the broader company architecture is still conceptual or mid-build
- the brand appears to still have elements of controlled access / semi-private launch behavior

There is also an important nuance here: the current public-facing experience seems polished in its language and philosophy, but still partially gated in practice. That means the internal system we build next is likely being created for a brand that is already speaking with clarity before all of its machinery is fully in place.

So this first internal building block should probably be understood in the context of a company that is already partially real, partially being assembled, and still defining how its systems connect.

## What Seems True So Far

Without deciding the dashboard yet, a few things seem clearly true:

- the internal system should probably relate to workstreams, departments, or operating layers rather than random features
- it should likely help Andrea see and direct AI execution
- it should probably respect Notion as source of truth rather than replace it

These are not final product decisions. They are just the current contextual read.
